The Savings Rule

A simple system that turns "just getting by" into building wealth. Three parts:
  • Pay yourself first: Every month, move a fixed amount into a separate savings account. Make it an AUTOMATIC transfer on payday — never rely on willpower.
  • Spend the rest freely: Whatever's left after that is genuinely yours to spend, guilt-free.
  • Keep it earning: Park savings in a separate high-interest account so it grows on its own.
Set your monthly savings amount in ⚙ Setup or 💬 Quick Update — the projection below uses it.

Why Automating It Works

A fixed automatic transfer, every month, compounds quietly in the background — no extra effort.
  • It removes the decision. Money you never see in your spending account never gets spent.
  • Small adds up fast. Even a modest monthly amount becomes a real cushion within a year.
  • Good months accelerate it. Any month you bank extra spare money (see the Cash Flow projection) gets you there faster.

    Klarna Float System

    A free, zero-interest cash tool — explained simply. With pay-in-2, Klarna sends the full amount to your bank now, and you pay it back over the next 2 months in two equal, interest-free instalments. Because there's no interest, you return exactly what you borrowed — it's really just bringing your own future salary forward to today, for free.
    ⚠ THE ONE RULE TO NEVER FORGET
    The money is NOT yours to keep — it's your own future salary pulled forward, and you return every bit over the next 2 months. Think "borrowing from myself, interest-free," never "free money." Treat it as keep-money and it will hurt you; treat it as a short bridge and it's a powerful free tool.

    How one cycle works (10k example)

    StepCash effect
    Klarna sends the full 10k to your bank+10,000
    In your hand right now (borrowed in full)10,000
    Month 1: repay 1st half (from salary)−5,000
    Month 2: repay 2nd half (from salary)−5,000
    End — exactly what you borrowed, returned0
    You get the whole 10k on day one and repay it interest-free over the next two months — 5,000 each — from salary that was arriving anyway. Total interest: zero; total repaid: exactly 10,000. The only thing that matters is that each instalment fits inside your monthly surplus (see Safe-Size below).

    "A Loan But Better" — The Buffer Build

    How to turn the short bridge into something permanent you actually own — costing nothing. Using an 8k example (comfortably inside a healthy surplus):
    • Step 1 — Borrow 8k from Klarna. You have the full 8k in your bank today.
    • Step 2 — DON'T spend it. Let it sit as a buffer.
    • Step 3 — Repay the two 4k instalments from your salary surplus over the next 2 months — not from the 8k you're holding.
    • Result — After 2 months Klarna is fully repaid and you still hold the original 8k, free and clear. You've simply pulled 2 months of saving forward into instant, interest-free liquidity — a buffer you got to use from day one at zero cost.

    Safe-Size Rule

    The bridge only works if you can cover each instalment from your salary surplus — and since repayment is split over 2 months, each instalment is half the amount borrowed. Your surplus on a typical month — income minus all recurring costs and any loan payment — is —.
    Amount borrowedInstalment (×2 months)Safe for you?
    Your comfortable ceiling is roughly the size where the half-instalment still fits inside your typical surplus. Treat anything that forces you to dip into savings to make an instalment as too big — it defeats the purpose.

    Honest Verdict

    Where this tool fits next to a real loan.
    • What it really is: interest-free borrowing from your own future salary — you receive the full amount now and return exactly that over 2 months. No more, no less.
    • What it's great for: free short-term breathing room — bridging a gap before payday, or pulling a buffer forward without touching savings.
    • What it can't do: replace a proper loan. It tops out at a small amount and must be cleared within 2 months. It resets to zero each cycle — it never builds a growing pile by itself.
    • The risk: Klarna has to approve you each time, and every bit must come back within 2 months. Never let it become something you need to survive the month.
    • Best use: a free bonus tool on top of your real plan — not the foundation of it.
